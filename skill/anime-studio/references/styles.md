# Style references

Name the reference, then say exactly what to borrow. Use references for craft and atmosphere
only: never copy a character, a shot or a design, and never show real logos or trademarks.

## Japanese theatrical anime, hand-painted era (Ghibli-era films)
- Backgrounds: gouache/watercolour, visible brushwork, layered foliage, warm natural light,
  colourful shadows, enormous skies.
- Characters: simple faces, clean ink line, cel colour with one hard shadow, expressive posture.
- Timing: long quiet holds, small realistic gestures, wind in grass and hair.
- Good for: rural, nostalgic, gentle stories ("Déserts", "Le Professeur Ostrogoth").

## Contemporary French adult animation ("J'ai perdu mon corps", Jérémy Clapin, 2019)
- Realistic, understated characters with long faces and real proportions; never big eyes.
- Sober, slightly rough line, sometimes open; flat muted colour, few tones.
- Naturalistic, almost documentary staging; grey light, cramped interiors full of lived-in objects;
  subjective framings and close-ups of hands.
- Good for: intimate, melancholy, urban stories.

## Classic French surrealist animation and bande dessinée (René Laloux's "La Planète sauvage", Moebius-era drawing)
- Fine ink hatching, pure flat colour fields, impossible organic forms, slow metamorphoses,
  dreamlike calm that unsettles.
- Good for: hallucinations, dreams, inner worlds (the "blue" in "Sérotonine bleue").

## Mixing styles in one film
Allowed when the story has two worlds (reality vs. dream, present vs. memory). Keep one shared
element (the line, the grain, a colour) so it still feels like one film.
